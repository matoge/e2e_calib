Dynamic-object σ behaviour of the cross-frame residual model

TL;DR: Although the model is never given any dynamic-object labels, the trained cross-frame residual model (v92, unified frame-token, multi-frame, c=4) spontaneously separates moving objects in its output σ. However, the magnitude of the increase is controlled, and especially for mid-range moving vehicles where "the image appearance can still somehow be matched", σ is not inflated and the model becomes over-confident.

Setup

  • model: experiments/cross_frame_v92_unified_multi_c4 (val_err=1.93 px, val_nll=1.93)
  • dataset: PandaSet 39-scene front_camera val split (~200 pair samples, ~32K query points)
  • analysis script: scripts/analysis/dynamic_object_variance.py

We take the world coordinates of each query point from the dataset (pts_w_A_query), and by checking whether each point falls inside a PandaSet 3D cuboid annotation, we classify:

label source data description
background not inside any cuboid road surface, buildings, vegetation, etc.
parked attributes.object_motion == 'Parked' parked vehicles
stopped attributes.object_motion == 'Stopped' waiting at a traffic light, etc.
moving attributes.object_motion == 'Moving' driving

into 4 categories, and aggregate the predicted σ and the actual residual |Δuv - GT|. For moving, we further subdivide into 4 buckets based on the A↔B displacement of the 3D box center:

bucket A↔B displacement
mv 0-1m 0 m ≤ d < 1 m (crawling / cornering)
mv 1-3m 1 m ≤ d < 3 m (normal driving)
mv 3-10m 3 m ≤ d < 10 m (medium speed, including oncoming traffic)
mv 10+m 10 m or more (fast oncoming traffic, vehicles leaving the view between frames)

Interpretation

✅ The first half of the hypothesis holds

  • parked and stopped are on par with background or lower (median 0.75 / 0.87 vs bg 0.87). Vehicle surfaces are texture-rich and easy to predict.
  • moving shows a clear rise in σ (1.07 vs 0.87). A "not confident in this prediction" signal is produced for dynamic objects.
  • Displacement and σ are also cleanly ordered from 0-1m → 1-3m: 0.78 → 1.30.

⚠ However the increase is modest, and it breaks down in the 3-10m bucket

  • mv 3-10m is the worst: residual mean 5.9 px / p90 15.4 px yet σ=1.33 → z = err/σ ≈ 4.4, a major calibration failure (over-confident).
  • In mv 10+m, σ actually drops (1.03). The model likely enters a "completely wrong, give up" mode.

🔍 Mechanism hypothesis

The model has learned "image appearance matching" under a static-scene assumption. Its behaviour by amount of motion is:

motion (px in image) model behaviour
< a few px below image-matching noise → treated as static and hit correctly, σ is low
5-15 px matching partially breaks down, σ rises a bit
> 20 px completely misses, but a visually plausible candidate exists → over-confident, σ is low

In other words, "σ is not raised as long as the appearance can carry it". This is correct from a calibration standpoint (if it is explainable as parallax, the static-scene assumption is fine). But as a dynamic-object detector there is an upper limit.

Structural observation gap

The dataset's inb filter (line 1097 of pandaset_pair.py) completely excludes from the val pool any point whose uv_hat or uv_gt falls outside the patch. Vehicles that have moved and completely left the view are never observed to begin with → cases where σ saturates at its full range (upper limit 30 px) cannot be observed.

Usability

With a σ > 1.5 px threshold it is practical as a moving-vehicle candidate mask (precision OK):

category share with σ > 1.5
background ~5% (false positive)
parked ~5%
stopped ~5%
moving ~30%
mv 1-3m ~40%

This is good enough for dynamic-object down-weighting as a front-end to the map maker / σ-weighted BA.

Limitations and next steps

Current limitations: - The ceiling of σ is fixed at around ~2-3 px through training (no motion-aware loss). - Moving vehicles outside the view cannot be observed. - Over-confident on medium-speed moving vehicles (3-10m bucket).

Improvement ideas: 1. Rewriting the GT: warping the points inside a moving vehicle by the box's motion makes "the warped location = true B position". This lets the model learn "appearance + motion compose". Semi-supervised. 2. Multi-frame consistency loss: if there are motion cues via M frames (visible in M but not in B), guide σ upward. 3. Relaxing the filter: remove inb so that the full range of σ can be observed (needed for BA evaluation).
